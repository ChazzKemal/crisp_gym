Entropy-guided demonstration acceleration · CoRL 2025

DemoSpeedup on the UR10e

A demonstration is not uniformly hard. Where the policy already knows what comes next, the demo can be replayed faster; where it does not — contacts, grasps, insertions — it must not be. This is that method, run end to end on our own recordings.

STEP 1

The proxy policy is an entropy oracle, not a product

Upstream trains an ACT on the original demonstrations and then uses it only to measure something. Nothing about that training run is specific to the method, which means any ACT checkpoint already trained on the dataset you want to accelerate will do. This run reuses one, so step 1 costs nothing.

What has to match is the feature layout — the oracle must have been trained on the observations it is about to be asked about.

Checkpoint × dataset

The proxy keeps its full 100-step chunk on purpose: a longer chunk gives the temporal aggregation in step 2 more overlapping predictions to pool per frame, and the entropy is computed over exactly that pool.

STEP 2

Ask the policy what it is unsure about

Replay each recorded demonstration through the proxy — the observations come from the demo, not from a rollout. At every frame, draw action chunks from the CVAE prior, pool the samples of every still-valid chunk that covers this frame, and take the KDE differential entropy of that cloud. With a 100-step chunk that is up to a thousand samples of the action now.

High entropy means many equally good continuations, which in practice means free motion. The valleys are where the policy commits to one answer — and those are the moments that must not be rushed.

STEP 3

Retime the episode, not the replay

The stride walk keeps one frame in two through precision phases and one in four elsewhere. Everything else is dropped — parquet rows and video frames alike — and what comes out is a stock LeRobot v3.0 dataset that lerobot-train reads without a patch.

The frame rate never changes. A retimed episode holds the same motion in half the frames, so replaying it at the original rate is what makes the arm move faster. Do not also raise the replay rate, or the acceleration lands twice.

Stride sweep — the number that decides whether this is safe

Upstream's (2, 4) is tuned on a simulated Aloha. On a real arm what matters is the last column: how fast the tool is commanded to move between two control periods.

Conversion checks

Segmenter backends do not agree

Upstream clusters (time, entropy) with HDBSCAN. Neither scikit-learn nor hdbscan is installed in lerobot-041, so the default backend is a numpy mean-split with a minimum run length. They are not interchangeable — pick one deliberately and record which.

STEP 4

Train it like any other ACT run

The only things that change are the dataset and the chunk size. Upstream halves it — 50 to 25 — because one accelerated frame covers two to four source frames, so half the chunk still spans about as much motion. Our baselines use 100, hence 50.
