Nine Steps,
Real Numbers

One recorded episode carried through every stage of the representation, printing the actual arrays and shapes at each point — including one B-spline evaluation small enough to check with a pencil.

    00The input

    Seven numbers, twenty times a second

    Everything starts from a recorded episode: a position, an axis-angle rotation, and a gripper command, once per control tick.

    The rotation is stored as a rotation vector: a direction giving the axis and a length giving the angle in radians. Compact, but a poor thing for a network to regress — it wraps at 2π and is discontinuous, so two nearly identical orientations can have very different numbers.

    01Rotation

    Why seven becomes ten

    The fix is the 6D continuous representation: expand the rotation to a matrix, keep its first two rows, and recover the third with a cross product when you need it back.

    Six numbers to carry three degrees of freedom is redundant on purpose. The redundancy is what makes it continuous, and it means a network's imperfect output still maps onto a valid rotation — Gram–Schmidt normalises the first row, removes its component from the second, and takes the cross product for the third.

    02The fit

    One spline over the whole episode

    A single cubic B-spline is fitted through all ten dimensions at once, with knots added until the worst error on any frame in any dimension falls under tolerance.

    Two things to read off. The knot vector opens with the boundary value repeated k+1 = 4 times, which is what clamps the curve to its endpoints. And the interior spacing runs from 1 to 6 frames — not uniform. The fitter spends knots where the trajectory is doing something and saves them where it is not. That unevenness is the entire economic argument for the method.

    03The window

    Slicing out one chunk

    A chunk is M = chunk_size + 2k consecutive knots plus the control points at the same indices. Nothing is refitted.

    Only the middle is usable. The three knots at each end are the overhang a cubic basis needs, so the valid domain is knots[3] to knots[22] — and the 26-knot vector admits exactly 26 − 3 − 1 = 22 control points, which is precisely what params[:-(degree+1)] slices off.

    04The matrix

    What the policy actually predicts

    Knots and control points are packed into one dense matrix: column 0 is the knot, columns 1–10 are the control point for that span.

    Note the control points are not poses on the path. Row 3's z = 0.5421 is a pull toward which the curve bends; the curve at frame 141 actually passes through 0.6188. That is why you cannot read a chunk as a list of waypoints without decoding it.

    05Time origin

    Making the knots read as "from now"

    Stored chunks subtract the current frame index from column 0, and only column 0. The control points never move.

    06Decode

    Back to waypoints

    Sample the curve across its valid domain, convert the rotation back to axis-angle, and compare against what was actually recorded at those frames.

    07By hand

    One evaluation, small enough to check

    All of the above rests on evaluating a B-spline. Here is de Boor's algorithm on a toy example you can follow with a pencil, then the same case through scipy.

    The recursion is a sequence of linear interpolations. Start with the k+1 control points whose basis functions are alive at x, then repeatedly blend neighbouring pairs, each time with a weight α given by where x sits between two knots. After k rounds one value is left, and it is the curve.

    Every α is a ratio of knot differences. That is the whole reason step 05 is free: add a constant to every knot and every numerator and denominator shifts by the same amount, so nothing changes.

    08The edge case

    Where the last waypoint goes

    The final chunk of an episode runs off the end of the knot vector, and gets padded by repeating the last knot. That has a precise consequence.

    Basis functions are supported on half-open intervals — closed on the left, open on the right. With the tail padded, every knot from index 22 onward is the same value, so at exactly x = tmax every basis function has already closed. They sum to 0 instead of 1, and the curve evaluates to the origin no matter what the control points say.

    Consequence

    Drop the final decoded waypoint of a padded chunk. It only occurs at the very end of an episode, and the decoder handles it — but decode a chunk yourself without knowing this and you get a command to drive the end effector to the base of the robot.
